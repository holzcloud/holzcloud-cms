<objective>
The settings, and the two ways this installation now refuses to start.

Nothing in this plan authenticates anybody. It writes the block of environment
variables the rest of the phase reads, it moves the listen address off every
interface, and it puts two refusals in the way of the configuration that would
ship the vulnerability.

**Why this is wave 1 and not a tracer slice.** GSD leads a phase with an
end-to-end tracer. It is overruled here for a concrete reason, not a stylistic
one: `ROADMAP.md`'s planning notes fix an eight-step build order whose steps are
"each step independently shippable and reversible", and its step ② is named *the
security core, before anything can rely on it*. A tracer that signs a person in
end-to-end before the peer gate exists is not a thin slice of the finished
system — it is the vulnerability, committed, with a test asserting it works. The
order stands, and every plan in this phase is one or two of its steps.

**The refusal that matters is the second one.**
`NewWebsiteAccessLookup` (`internal/admin/handler.go`) reads `return assigned == 0 || mine > 0`. "No
assignment means every website" is right for accounts an operator creates by
hand and it **inverts** under auto-provisioning: a freshly provisioned account
has zero `user_websites` rows by construction, so the first stranger who
authenticates gets editor access to every website in the installation. That line
is not touched — changing it would lock out every existing editor. The fix is
here, at the other end: provisioning off unless switched on, and switched on
without a named default website the process does not start. `config.go:133`'s
Payrexx pair is the shape, and the sentence beside it — "half a configuration is
worse than none" — is the reason, unchanged.

Purpose: SSO-10 in full, SSO-04's "kept in the environment rather than the
database", and SSO-05's "refuses to start".
Output: nine settings, four refusals, and a listen address that is no longer
every interface.
</objective>
