<objective>
The criterion that is easy to declare and easy to get wrong, and Phase 7 already
got it wrong once in this exact place.

`internal/bundle/format.go:163-170` states the rule the whole design rests on: a
reusable thing crosses the manifest **by its name**, "not the slug because a
manifest exists to be read and repaired by hand, and because the reading side
has always worked this way". `export.go:518-534` translates a slug out to a
name; `import.go:573-583` derives the slug back with `page.Slugify`.

**An album has a slug that `Rename` does not move** — that is plan 11-02's
design and it is deliberate, because the slug is what every page carrying the
album points at and correcting a typo must not break them. So after a rename the
page holds the *old* slug while the album shows a *new* name. If the block's
reference travels as a slug, the other machine derives a different slug from the
name and the reference points at nothing — **silently**, with an empty gallery
where the pictures were.

Which is why the proving test renames first, and why `bundle_test.go:1124-1137`
spends thirteen lines saying so: an album whose name still matches its slug
round-trips correctly *even when the translation is missing*, so a test without
the rename proves nothing at all. That paragraph is about a Term field and every
word of it applies here.

**Four pieces, and all four are required or this is a silent data loss** (§D.2):
the `Album` struct on the manifest with its pictures as file names,
`exportAlbums`, `importAlbums`, and the translation of a gallery block's
reference. Plan 11-05 already carries the block's album value across the archive
as a **pass-through**, so the value is never quietly dropped; this plan replaces
that pass-through with the translation and is where GAL-04 becomes true.

Purpose: GAL-04 in full.
Output: an album exported, renamed before it went, imported into a fresh
website, and every page that carried it still carrying it.
</objective>
